How to write a homepage that sells

A homepage should answer one question fast: can you help me? Use this simple structure and these writing rules to turn visitors into customers.

By Zentorum Agency5 min readUpdated 1 Nov 2026

Your homepage has one job: to help the right visitor decide that you are the answer to their problem, and to take the next step. Most homepages fail because they talk about the business instead of the customer. Here is a simple way to write one that works, with no special writing skills needed.

Start with the customer, not with you

Visitors arrive with a question: "Can this business help me?" They scan, they do not read. So your words must answer fast. Before you write anything, answer these three questions in one sentence each:

  1. Who is my ideal customer?
  2. What problem do they have?
  3. How do I solve it, and what is the result for them?

Example: "We help busy families get a clean home without the stress. Book in two minutes and come home to a fresh space." That is clear, human and specific.

The structure of a homepage that sells

You do not need a fancy layout. A simple order works for almost every small business.

1. The headline (top of the page)

This is the most important line. It should say what you do and for whom, in plain words. Avoid slogans that could belong to anyone. "Quality service you can trust" says nothing. "Wedding photos that feel like you" says something.

Tips:

  • Keep it under about 12 words.
  • Name the benefit, not the tool.
  • Skip jargon.

2. The sub-headline

One or two sentences that add detail: what you offer, how it works or why it is different.

3. The main button

Tell people exactly what to do: "Get a free quote", "Book a call", "See our work". Make it stand out in color and place it where the eye lands first. Repeat it lower on the page.

4. A short list of what you offer

Three to five items, each with a title and one line. Link each to a longer page. Keep it simple, so people see their need quickly. Our guide to 10 things every small business website needs shows what these pages should hold.

5. Proof

Claims are cheap. Proof is powerful. Use real customer quotes, photos of finished work, logos of clients, or the number of years you have been in business. Only use true facts and real testimonials with permission. Never invent numbers or quotes. Reviews also help your search presence, as covered in how to get more Google reviews.

6. How it works

Show the path in three simple steps, such as "Tell us what you need, we send a plan, we deliver." This removes fear of the unknown.

7. About you, briefly

People buy from people. A short note with a photo and a line about why you do this builds trust. Keep it to a few sentences and link to your full About page.

8. Answers to common questions

Add a few questions that stop people from buying: price, timing, what is included, what if I am not happy. Answer honestly.

9. A final call to action

End with a clear invitation and your contact details. Many visitors reach the bottom ready to act. Do not leave them stranded.

Writing rules that make words sell

  • Use "you" more than "we". Count them. If "we" wins, rewrite.
  • Write short sentences. One idea per sentence.
  • Be specific. "Delivered in 3 to 7 days" beats "fast".
  • Use everyday words. If you would not say it to a friend, do not write it.
  • Lead with benefits. Features say what it is. Benefits say what it does for the customer.
  • Avoid hype. Words like "best", "amazing" and "revolutionary" make people doubt you.

Design helps the words work

Good layout makes text easier to scan: plenty of white space, short paragraphs, clear headings and one main button per section. The page must also work on phones, where most people will see it. See our mobile-first guide and, if you plan a page just for one offer, landing page anatomy.

A quick before-and-after

Before: "Welcome to Smith Cleaning. We are a family business with many years of experience offering a wide range of quality services."

After: "A clean home, without the stress. Book in two minutes and we handle the rest."

The second version talks to the customer, names a benefit and points to an action. You can rewrite your own headline the same way in ten minutes.

Test your homepage in 5 seconds

Show it to someone who does not know your business for five seconds, then hide it. Ask them:

  1. What does this business do?
  2. Who is it for?
  3. What would you click next?

If they cannot answer, simplify the top of the page.

Checklist

  • My headline says what I do and for whom.
  • I have one main call to action, repeated.
  • I list my services simply.
  • I show real proof.
  • I explain how it works.
  • I answer the top questions.
  • My contact details are easy to find.
  • I use "you" more than "we".
  • It reads well on a phone.

Do not forget the brand

Words, logo and colors should feel like one voice. If your visuals are not settled yet, look at how to choose brand colors.

Quick questions

What should be at the top of my homepage?

A clear headline that says what you do and who it is for, a short supporting line and one obvious button such as Get a quote or Book a call.

How long should a homepage be?

Long enough to answer a visitor's main questions, and no longer. Most work well with five to nine short sections.

Do I need testimonials on my homepage?

They help a lot. Use real quotes with permission, and never invent reviews or numbers.
