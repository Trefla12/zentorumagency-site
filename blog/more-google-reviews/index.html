How to get more Google reviews, the honest way

Reviews help new customers trust you. Here is how to earn more of them honestly, with simple scripts, a direct link and a routine that fits a busy week.

By Zentorum Agency5 min readUpdated 1 Nov 2026

When someone searches for a business like yours, the stars and reviews are often the first thing they see. They help people decide who to trust before they ever visit your website.

There are shortcuts out there: bought reviews, fake accounts, discounts for five stars. Do not use them. They break Google's rules, can get reviews removed, and can harm your reputation. This guide covers the honest way, and it works.

What you need first

Reviews appear on your Google Business Profile, which is the free listing that shows on Google Search and Maps. If you have not claimed or completed yours, start there. Our guide to setting up a Google Business Profile walks you through it.

Make sure your name, address or service area, hours and phone number are correct. Reviews on a half-finished profile do less for you.

Step 1: Get your direct review link

The biggest reason customers do not leave a review is friction. If they have to search for your business and figure out where to click, most will give up.

  1. Sign in to your Google Business Profile.
  2. Look for the option to ask for reviews or share a review form.
  3. Copy the short link.
  4. Save it in your phone notes so you can paste it quickly.

You can also turn this link into a QR code and print it. It is a simple way to make the step easy at a counter, on a receipt or on a business card.

Step 2: Ask at the right moment

The best time to ask is right after a good moment: the customer has just received the result, thanked you, or smiled. That is when they feel most positive and the experience is fresh.

  • After a job is finished and the customer is happy.
  • When someone sends a thank-you message.
  • After a successful delivery or a completed project.
  • At the end of a visit, if you are a local shop.

Do not wait weeks. People forget, and the details fade.

Step 3: Use a simple, human script

Keep the request short and personal. Here are two you can copy and adjust.

In person: "I'm really glad you're happy with it. If you have a minute, a short review on Google would help other people find us. I can send you the link right now."
By text or email: "Hi [Name], thanks again for choosing us. If you enjoyed working with us, would you leave a short review? Here is the link: [link]. It really helps a small business like ours."

Notice what is not there: no promise of a gift, no pressure, no request for a particular star rating. You are simply asking for an honest opinion.

What not to do

  • Do not buy reviews. This is against Google's rules, and fake reviews are easy for readers to spot.
  • Do not write your own. Not for you, and not for friends who have never used your service.
  • Do not offer discounts, gifts or prizes in exchange. Incentivized reviews break Google's policies.
  • Do not only ask happy customers. Filtering who gets the link ("review gating") is also against the rules.
  • Do not post reviews for competitors or leave negative fake ones. It is dishonest and can cause legal trouble.

Reviews are worth far more when they are real. A few honest reviews beat a pile of suspicious ones.

Step 4: Reply to every review

Replying shows that a real person is behind the business. It also gives you another chance to show your tone.

For good reviews

Thank the person by name, mention something specific, and keep it short. Avoid copying the same reply every time.

For bad reviews

Wait before you answer. Then follow this pattern:

  1. Thank them for the feedback.
  2. Apologize for the part that went wrong, if it did.
  3. Offer a way to fix it: a phone number or email.
  4. Keep it calm, short and free of arguments.

Future customers will read how you handle criticism. A calm answer can build more trust than a perfect rating.

Step 5: Build it into your routine

Reviews rarely come from one big push. They come from a small habit repeated over many weeks.

  • Add the review link to your email signature.
  • Put it in your follow-up message after every job.
  • Add a small sign or QR code at your counter or on invoices.
  • Mention it on your website's thank-you page.
  • Set a weekly reminder to message the customers you finished with that week.

If you have a website, add a link or a button to your reviews page there as well. Our list of website must-haves includes trust signals like this.

Do reviews help your Google ranking?

Reviews are widely seen as one of several things that help local businesses stand out, and they clearly help customers decide. But nobody can guarantee a #1 spot, and anyone who says they can is not being honest. Treat reviews as one part of your overall visibility. If you want to understand the bigger picture, start with what SEO is.

A simple monthly checklist

  1. Check for new reviews and reply to all of them.
  2. Send the review link to each customer you finished with.
  3. Look for patterns in feedback and fix real problems.
  4. Make sure your profile details are still correct.
  5. Share one great review on social media, with the customer's permission if you use their name.

Quick questions

Can I offer a discount for a review?

No. Google's policies do not allow reviews in exchange for rewards, and it can lead to reviews being removed. Ask politely and let customers decide.

What do I do about a bad review?

Stay calm, reply politely and offer to fix the problem offline. Future customers read your reply as much as the review itself.

Can I ask customers for only positive reviews?

No. Ask everyone who had an experience with you, and let them say what they think. Filtering out unhappy customers breaks Google's policies.
