What makes a good logo?

A good logo is simple, memorable and works everywhere. Learn the principles, the mistakes to avoid and what files you should receive.

By Zentorum Agency5 min readUpdated 1 Nov 2026

A logo is often the first thing a customer sees. It sits on your website, your cards, your packaging and your social profiles. A good one makes you look professional and easy to remember. A weak one makes people forget you. But what makes a logo "good"? Here are the principles, in plain English.

A good logo is simple

Think of the best-known logos you can picture: most have a simple shape, one or two colors and almost no detail. Simple logos are easy to recognize at a glance, easy to remember and easy to draw from memory.

Test: can someone describe your logo after seeing it for three seconds? If not, simplify.

A good logo is memorable

A logo should have one distinctive idea: a clever shape, a unique letter style or a symbol that links to what you do. Avoid generic clip-art ideas, such as a globe for "global" or a handshake for "trust". If thousands of other businesses use the same idea, you will blend in.

A good logo is timeless

Trends come and go. A logo built on a fashion of the moment can look dated within a few years. Aim for a clean design that will still look right in ten years. You can always freshen it later, but constantly redesigning confuses customers.

A good logo is versatile

Your logo must work everywhere, large and small. Check it in these situations:

  • As a tiny icon, like a social media profile picture or a browser tab.
  • On a large sign or banner.
  • In one color only, such as black on white.
  • On light and dark backgrounds.
  • Printed on a stamp, invoice or packaging.
  • Next to your business name in different layouts, wide and stacked.

If it falls apart in any of these, it is not finished.

A good logo is appropriate

The style should suit your business and your customers. A children's toy shop and a law firm need different feelings. A logo does not have to show what you sell, but it should feel right. Think about the personality of your brand: friendly, serious, playful, premium, calm.

Color and type matter

Color

Colors set the mood, and they should work together. Keep it to one or two main colors. Make sure there is enough contrast so the logo is readable. See how to choose brand colors for a step-by-step approach.

Typography

Typography means the style of letters. Choose a font that is clear and fits your personality. Avoid decorative fonts that are hard to read at small sizes, and do not mix more than two styles.

Common logo mistakes

  • Too much detail. Fine lines vanish when small.
  • Too many colors. It gets expensive to print and hard to use.
  • Copying another brand. It is risky and weakens your identity.
  • Using stock templates. Others may use the same one.
  • Relying on effects. Shadows, glows and gradients often fail in print and in one color.
  • Using the wrong file type. A low-quality image looks blurry when enlarged.
  • Making it by committee. Too many opinions lead to a bland result.

What should you receive?

A professional logo job should deliver more than one picture. Ask for:

  1. The main logo, plus stacked and horizontal versions.
  2. A small icon version.
  3. Full color, one color, black and white.
  4. Files for print and for screen, including a scalable vector file.
  5. Basic guidance on colors and fonts, a "mini brand kit".
  6. Full ownership of the final files.

With Zentorum Agency, a logo and mini brand kit costs $300 to $500 and takes 1 to 4 days. It includes 2 rounds of changes, and you own the final files. See our logo design service. If you want it to move, a motion logo (an animated version for video and social) costs $150 to $500.

How to brief a designer

You will get a better logo if you explain your needs clearly. Share who your customers are, three adjectives for your brand, logos you like and dislike, and where it will be used. Our guide how to brief a designer has a full list.

Does your logo fit your website?

A logo is only the start of your identity. It should sit well in your header, feel consistent with your colors and photography, and work in your social profiles. If you are planning a site, think about the logo first, and see 10 things every small business website needs. It also helps to choose your domain name in line with your brand.

Redesign or refresh?

If you already have a logo, you do not always need a new one. Ask whether the problem is the idea or the execution. A messy file, a poor color choice or an outdated font can often be fixed with a refresh that keeps the recognition you have built. Start over only if the logo no longer fits your business or fails the tests above.

Quick logo test

Give your logo one point for each yes:

  • It is simple enough to draw from memory.
  • It looks good very small.
  • It works in one color.
  • It works on light and dark backgrounds.
  • It fits my customers and my brand personality.
  • It does not look like another brand.
  • It will still look right in ten years.
  • I have the editable and print-ready files.

Seven or eight: well done. Five or six: it needs some polish. Under five: consider a redesign.

Final thoughts

A good logo is not about being clever or complicated. It is about being clear, memorable and useful in every place your customers meet you. Invest the time to get it right, and it will serve your business for years.

Which logo files should I get?
