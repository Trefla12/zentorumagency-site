Website or social media: do you need both?

Social media brings attention. A website brings trust and sales. Here is how a small business can use both on a sensible budget.

By Zentorum Agency6 min readUpdated 1 Nov 2026

Many owners ask us the same question: "Do I really need a website if I already have Instagram or Facebook?" It is a fair question. Social media is free to start, quick to set up and full of people. So why spend money on a website?

The short answer: social media is great for being seen, and a website is great for being trusted and found. Most small businesses do best with both, each doing its own job.

What social media does well

  • Reaches people fast. You can post today and be seen today.
  • Shows your personality. Photos, short videos and stories make your business feel human.
  • Builds a community. People can comment, share and message you.
  • Costs little to start. A free account is enough to begin.

Short videos work especially well. If you want a simple routine, see our weekly plan for reels.

What a website does well

  • You own it. Your page on a social platform belongs to that platform. Rules, layouts and reach can change without warning. Your website is yours.
  • It shows up in search. People who search for what you sell can find a website. Social posts are much harder to find later.
  • It builds trust. Many customers check for a website before they buy. No site can make a business look smaller or less serious.
  • It works as a sales page. You decide what visitors see and what they do next, without ads or distractions.
  • It never gets buried. A post disappears down the feed in a day. A page on your website stays put.

When social media alone may be enough

There are cases where starting with social media only makes sense:

  • You are testing a very early idea.
  • Your customers come only from word of mouth and local groups.
  • Your budget is close to zero right now.

Even then, treat it as a first step. Plan to build a website once the idea works.

When you need a website

A website becomes important when:

  • You want to be found by people searching on Google.
  • You sell services that need trust, like design, repairs, coaching or consulting.
  • You want to take bookings, payments or enquiries in one place.
  • You work with other businesses, who often check a site first.
  • You want to run ads and send people somewhere you control.

If you are not sure what to put on the site, read 10 things every small business website needs.

How the two work together

Think of it as a shop and a street. Social media is the street: busy, noisy, full of people walking by. Your website is the shop: calm, organized, and built to help someone buy. You need the street to attract people, and the shop to serve them.

A simple flow

  1. Someone sees your reel or post.
  2. They tap the link in your bio.
  3. They land on a clear page that explains what you do.
  4. They book, call or buy.

If step 3 is missing, you lose people at the moment they are interested. A page that explains your offer clearly matters. Learn how in how to write a homepage that sells.

How to split your time and money

You do not need to be everywhere. Use this simple approach.

  1. Start with the basics. A simple website with your services, contact details and a few reviews.
  2. Pick one or two social platforms. Choose where your customers already spend time.
  3. Post on a schedule you can keep. Three good posts a week beat daily posts you give up on.
  4. Link both ways. Put your website in every profile, and social links on your site.
  5. Look at what works. Keep doing more of it.

If posting every week feels like too much, our social media service ($500 to $1,500 per month) can take it off your plate.

Common mistakes

  • Using a social page as your only home. If the account is lost or restricted, so is your business presence.
  • Building a website and never promoting it. A site needs visitors. Share it, and consider SEO.
  • Different looks everywhere. Use the same logo, colors and tone on both. See how to choose brand colors.
  • Copying big brands. Do what suits your size and your customers.

A quick example

Imagine a small bakery. On social media, it shares photos of fresh bread and short videos of the morning bake. People love it and follow along. But when a new customer searches for "custom wedding cakes", they find nothing, because there is no website with a page about cakes, prices and how to order.

Now add a simple site with a cakes page, a few photos and an enquiry form. The same social posts now lead somewhere. Search visitors can find the bakery. Customers can ask for a quote at midnight without waiting for a reply. Same business, same products, better results from the same effort.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Where do my customers look for businesses like mine?
  • Would I trust a business that has no website?
  • Can people book or buy from me in one clear step?
  • How much time can I truly give to posting each week?

Final thoughts

You do not have to choose. Social media brings attention, and a website turns attention into trust and sales. If you can only do one at first, pick based on your goal: fast visibility, social; lasting credibility, website. Then add the other as soon as you can.

Quick questions

Can I run my business only on social media?

You can start that way, but you do not own the account. A website gives you a stable home that you control and that people can find through search.

Which should I do first, website or social media?

If you need fast attention, start with social media. If you need trust and to be found on Google, start with a website. Most businesses add the second soon after.

Do I need to be on every social platform?

No. Pick one or two where your customers already spend time and post on a schedule you can keep.
