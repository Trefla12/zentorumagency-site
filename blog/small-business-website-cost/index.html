How much does a small business website cost in 2026?

Real price ranges for a small business website, the hidden costs people forget, and how to set a budget that makes sense.

By Zentorum Agency6 min readUpdated 1 Nov 2026

Ask five people what a small business website costs and you will get five different answers. That is because "a website" can mean a simple one-page site or a full online store. This guide gives you real price ranges, explains what drives the cost, and shows you how to avoid paying for things you do not need.

The short answer

For most small businesses, a professional website costs from about $1,250 for a simple site of 1 to 5 pages. A larger site with 6 to 15 pages, or an online store, starts around $2,500. Add small yearly costs for your domain and hosting, and you have the full picture.

These are our own prices at Zentorum Agency. Other agencies and freelancers will charge differently, but the pattern is the same: more pages and more features cost more.

What affects the price

Number of pages

Every page needs writing, layout, images and testing. Five pages take less work than fifteen.

Custom design or a template

A template is cheaper because the layout already exists. A custom design is built around your brand and your customers. It costs more, but it looks like you and not like everyone else.

Features

A contact form is simple. Online booking, a store, member logins or a multi-language site add work. Each feature should earn its place by helping you get customers.

Words and images

If you send us ready text and photos, the job is faster. If you need help with writing, product photos or 3D renders, that adds cost. You can see what we offer on our web design page.

Your brand

If you do not have a logo or colors yet, it is smart to sort them out first. A logo and mini brand kit costs $300 to $500 and makes the website look consistent.

The costs people forget

The build price is not the whole bill. Plan for these as well.

  • Domain name: about $10 to $20 per year for most common endings. See our guide to choosing a domain name.
  • Hosting: about $5 to $30 per month for a small site, depending on the plan.
  • Business email: about $5 to $10 per user per month if you want you@yourbusiness.com.
  • Maintenance: updates, backups and security checks. Our website maintenance checklist shows what is involved.
  • Marketing: a website does not bring visitors by itself. SEO costs $200 to $1,200 per month with us, with a 2-month minimum. Read what SEO is before you decide.

These third-party prices are rough ranges. Always check the current price before you buy.

DIY builder, freelancer or agency?

Do it yourself

Website builders are cheap, often about $15 to $40 per month. You pay with your time. If you enjoy it and your needs are simple, it can work. If you are busy, the site often stays half finished.

A freelancer

A good freelancer can be great value. The risk is that one person does everything, so a busy week or a sick day can slow your project.

An agency

An agency gives you a team: design, writing help, SEO and branding under one roof. You pay more than DIY, but you save time and avoid common mistakes.

Want your own numbers? Our DIY vs freelancer vs agency calculator adds up the real cost, including your time.

Why very cheap sites cost more later

A very low price often hides something. The site may use a copied template, load slowly, break on phones or give you no ownership of the files. Fixing that later can cost more than doing it right the first time. If your site feels slow, see why websites are slow and how to fix it.

Ask every provider these questions before you sign:

  1. Do I own the final files and the domain?
  2. How many rounds of changes are included?
  3. Will the site work well on phones?
  4. What happens after launch? Is there support?
  5. Are there any monthly fees I will have to pay you?

With us, every package includes 2 rounds of changes, and you own the final files.

How to set a sensible budget

Follow these steps.

  1. Write down the goal. Do you want phone calls, bookings, or online sales?
  2. List the pages you really need. Most small businesses need Home, About, Services and Contact.
  3. Collect what you already have. Logo, photos, text, reviews.
  4. Decide what can wait. A blog or a store can be added in phase two.
  5. Keep money for traffic. A website nobody visits earns nothing.

Quick checklist before you hire

  • I know my main goal for the site.
  • I have a fixed price in writing.
  • I know the delivery time.
  • I know who owns the files.
  • I know the yearly cost of domain and hosting.
  • I have a plan to get visitors.

Not sure what to include? Our guide to the things every small business website needs is a good place to start.

Final thoughts

A website is one of the best long-term tools a small business can own. It works while you sleep, answers questions and builds trust. Spend enough to do it well, keep the scope focused, and add more later as the business grows.

Quick questions

How much should a small business spend on a website?

Most small businesses spend from about $1,250 for a simple site of 1 to 5 pages. Larger sites and online stores start around $2,500. Add small yearly costs for the domain and hosting.

Are there monthly costs after the website is built?

Usually yes, but they are small. Expect hosting, a domain renewal and possibly business email. SEO and maintenance are optional extras.

Do I own my website once it is finished?

You should. With Zentorum Agency, you own the final files. Always ask any provider this before signing.
