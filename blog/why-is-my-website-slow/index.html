Why your website is slow, and how to fix it

Big images, crowded hosting and too many plugins are the usual causes of a slow website. Learn how to measure and fix each one.

By Zentorum Agency6 min readUpdated 1 Nov 2026

A slow website costs you customers. Visitors get impatient, tap the back button and go to a competitor. The good news is that most slow sites have a few common causes, and many are easy to fix. This guide shows you how to find the problem and what to do about it.

Why speed matters

  • Visitors leave. People expect pages to appear almost at once, especially on a phone.
  • Trust drops. A slow site feels old or unsafe.
  • Search engines notice. Speed is one of many things search engines consider. We cannot promise rankings, but a faster site helps both users and search. See what SEO is.
  • Sales suffer. If a page takes too long, fewer people reach the contact form or the checkout.

First, measure it

Do not guess. Run a free speed test and write down the results so you can compare later. Free tools exist from Google (PageSpeed Insights) and others. Also try this simple test:

  1. Open your site on your phone using mobile data, not Wi-Fi.
  2. Count how long until you can read the main headline.
  3. Count how long until you can tap a button.
  4. Do this on your homepage and one other page.

If it feels slow to you, it feels slow to your customers.

The most common causes, and the fixes

1. Images that are too big

This is the number one cause. A photo straight from a camera or phone can be several megabytes. Your page needs only a fraction of that.

Fix:

  • Resize images to the size they are actually shown.
  • Compress them using a free tool.
  • Use modern formats such as WebP where possible.
  • Load images below the visible area only when the visitor scrolls to them ("lazy loading").

2. Cheap or crowded hosting

Hosting is the computer that stores your site. Very cheap plans often share a server with hundreds of other sites, which can slow yours down.

Fix: move to a better plan or a provider known for speed. A decent small site plan often costs about $5 to $30 per month. Check the current price before you buy.

3. Too many plugins or add-ons

On platforms like WordPress, every plugin adds code. Some are heavy, and many are never used.

Fix: list your plugins, delete those you do not need, and replace heavy ones with lighter options.

4. Heavy themes and page builders

Some themes are packed with features you will never use, all of which load on every page.

Fix: choose a lean theme, or have a custom, lightweight design. This is one reason we build custom. See our web design service.

5. Videos and animations

An autoplaying video at the top of a page can be huge. Fancy animations can also slow older phones.

Fix: host videos on a video platform and embed them, compress them well, and do not autoplay big files. Keep the page simple. If you use video, our video editing tips include how to export smaller files.

6. Third-party scripts

Chat widgets, tracking tools, pop-ups and social feeds all load code from other servers. Each one adds delay.

Fix: keep only the ones that earn their place and remove the rest.

7. No caching

Caching saves a ready-made copy of your pages so they do not have to be rebuilt for every visitor.

Fix: turn on caching through your hosting or a trusted caching tool.

8. Too many fonts

Each font style is another file to download.

Fix: use one or two fonts, and only the weights you need.

9. Old or messy code

Sites that have been patched for years often carry leftover code.

Fix: a clean-up or rebuild. Regular care helps, too. See the website maintenance checklist.

A simple order of work

  1. Test and record your starting speed.
  2. Compress and resize images.
  3. Remove unused plugins and scripts.
  4. Turn on caching.
  5. Replace autoplay video with a click-to-play version.
  6. Test again and compare.
  7. If it is still slow, look at hosting or the theme.

Steps 2 and 3 alone often make a big difference.

Mobile first

Most visitors use phones, often on slower connections than your office Wi-Fi. Always test on a phone, and design with small screens in mind. We explain how in our mobile-first guide.

When to fix and when to rebuild

If speed problems come from a few large images, fix them. If your site is slow everywhere, built on a heavy theme and full of old add-ons, a rebuild may cost less than endless patching. Compare options in how much a small business website costs.

Prevent it from happening again

  • Compress every image before you upload it.
  • Ask before adding a new plugin: do we really need it?
  • Test speed after every big change.
  • Review your site every few months.

What a good result looks like

You do not need a perfect score from a test tool. Aim for a page where the main content appears in a couple of seconds on a phone and buttons respond right away. Compare your results with your own earlier numbers, and with a competitor if you like. Progress matters more than a perfect number, and every second you save helps real visitors.

Speed checklist

  • Images are resized, compressed and lazy loaded.
  • Hosting is reliable and not overcrowded.
  • Unused plugins are gone.
  • Caching is on.
  • Videos do not autoplay at full size.
  • Only one or two fonts.
  • I tested on a real phone.

Quick questions

What is the most common reason a website is slow?

Oversized images. Resizing and compressing them is usually the quickest and biggest improvement.

Does a slow website hurt my search results?

Speed is one of many things search engines consider, and slow pages also make visitors leave. Nobody can guarantee rankings, but a fast site helps.

Can I fix a slow site myself?

Often yes. Compress images, delete unused plugins and turn on caching. If it is still slow, the cause may be hosting or the theme, and a professional can help.
