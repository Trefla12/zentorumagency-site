How long does it take to build a website?

Most small business websites take days, not months. See the steps, what causes delays and how to speed up your project.

By Zentorum Agency5 min readUpdated 1 Nov 2026

"How long until my website is ready?" is one of the first questions we hear. The honest answer is: it depends. But it depends on a few clear things, and once you know them, you can plan well and even speed things up yourself.

The short answer

At Zentorum Agency, a website of 1 to 5 pages takes about 3 to 7 days. A website of 6 to 15 pages, or an online store, takes about 7 to 11 days. Those times assume we have what we need from you quickly.

Other providers may take weeks or months. The difference is rarely the design work. It is usually waiting for text, photos and feedback.

The steps of a website project

  1. Brief and planning. We learn about your business, your customers and your goal. We agree on the pages.
  2. Content. Text, photos, logo and any other material are gathered or created.
  3. Design. We create the look: layout, colors, fonts, images.
  4. Build. The design becomes a working website that fits phones, tablets and computers.
  5. Review. You look at it and ask for changes. Every package includes 2 rounds of changes.
  6. Testing and launch. We check links, forms, speed and mobile layout. Then the site goes live.

What makes a project faster

Ready content

Content is the number one cause of delay. If you have your text, photos and logo ready on day one, the project moves fast. If you need to write everything from scratch, plan extra time. Our guide on how to write a homepage that sells will help you draft quickly.

A clear goal

"I want a nice website" is hard to build. "I want people to book a free call" is easy to build. A single clear goal means fewer debates and fewer redesigns.

Fast feedback

Every day a draft sits in an inbox is a day added to the schedule. Try to reply within a day or two.

One decision maker

When three partners each want different changes, rounds of feedback multiply. Choose one person who gives the final answer.

What makes a project slower

  • Missing content. The site is built, but the text is not written yet.
  • Too many pages. Each extra page adds writing, design and testing.
  • Special features. Booking systems, stores with many products, and multi-language sites take more time.
  • No brand yet. If you still need a logo and colors, that comes first. Our logo design service takes 1 to 4 days.
  • Endless changes. Small tweaks are fine. Starting over is not.

Why a rushed site can be a bad idea

Speed is great, but not at any price. A good website still needs thought: who is it for, what should they do, and why should they trust you? Skipping that step may give you a fast launch and a slow flow of customers.

That said, you do not need months. A focused site launched this month beats a perfect site launched next year. You can always improve it later.

Your pre-project checklist

Do these things before the project starts, and you will save days.

  • Write one sentence about what the website should achieve.
  • List the pages you want.
  • Gather your logo, photos and any product or service descriptions.
  • Collect 3 to 5 customer reviews or testimonials.
  • Pick 2 or 3 websites you like and note why.
  • Decide on your domain name. See how to choose a domain name that works.
  • Choose who gives the final approval.

Not sure how to explain what you want? Our guide on how to brief a designer can help.

What happens after launch

Launch day is not the finish line. Right after launch, check that your forms work, your phone number is right and the site looks good on your own phone. Then think about getting found: set up a Google Business Profile and consider SEO. Search results do not appear overnight, and nobody can guarantee a number one spot, but good habits add up over time.

Common questions about timing

Does a bigger budget mean a faster site?

Not always. More money can buy more pages or features, but the biggest delays come from content and feedback. A focused site with ready content will usually beat a big site with missing text.

Can the site go live in stages?

Yes, and it is often smart. Launch the core pages first: Home, Services, About and Contact. Add a blog, gallery or store once the first version is live and working.

What if I need changes after launch?

Small updates are normal. Every package includes 2 rounds of changes, and the final files are yours. For ongoing care, keep a simple routine and follow our maintenance checklist.

Final thoughts

A website does not have to take months. With a clear goal, ready content and quick feedback, a good small business site can be live in about a week. The more you prepare, the faster and cheaper the whole process becomes.
